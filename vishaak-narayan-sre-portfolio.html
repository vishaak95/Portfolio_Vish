01 · Approach

Reliability is a product feature, not a pager rota.

I started in 24/7 production support for core banking applications. Nothing teaches you the real cost of downtime faster than being the first responder at 3 a.m. It also teaches you that restarting a service is not the same as fixing it.

Home lending adds its own weight: a loan application, a monthly payment and a secondary-market trade each depend on systems that must be correct and available. As a technology consultant I work across originations, servicing and capital markets, translating business priorities into reliability targets and engineering work that engineers and executives can both act on.

Since then I’ve moved from reacting to engineering. I design observability across Splunk, Dynatrace, Grafana and Prometheus, lead high-severity incident response, write Python and shell automation that retires manual toil, and work with Kubernetes, AWS, Terraform and CI/CD to make change safe.

Just as important is the human side. I run blameless post-mortems, mentor junior engineers, and translate SLIs, SLOs and SLAs into the language product owners and vendors actually use.

The best incident is the one customers never hear about.

How I work

Measure what users feel

Start from service-level objectives tied to business outcomes, then work backwards to the alerts worth waking someone for.

Automate the second time

Do it once by hand to learn it. The second time, it becomes a script, a runbook or a self-healing workflow.

Blameless, not toothless

Post-mortems find system causes and produce tracked fixes with owners. Culture and accountability reinforce each other.

Quiet is the goal

Less noise, fewer false positives, calmer on-call. Good observability should make teams faster, not busier.
